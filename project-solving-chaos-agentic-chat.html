The Problem

The Broken Workflow

Investment banking analysts spent 60-70% of their time collecting data, formatting reports, and managing versions—not analyzing. A single institutional-grade report required 6-8 hours of manual work:

  • Data Collection: 2-3 hours pulling from Bloomberg, CapIQ, PitchBook, FactSet
  • Organization & Cleaning: 1.5-2 hours standardizing formats, cross-referencing
  • Report Assembly: 1-1.5 hours building PowerPoint, Excel, Word documents
  • Review & Versioning: 1-2 hours managing feedback and revisions
  • Compliance Check: 30-45 minutes ensuring regulatory alignment

Result: Analysts produced only 1-2 reports per week, with only 30-40% of their time spent on actual analysis.

Business Opportunity

The financial incentive was clear:

  • Institutions pay analysts $150K-$300K annually
  • Report-writing overhead: $36-48K per analyst per year (30-40% of salary)
  • Across 15 institutional clients × 50 analysts = $30M annual inefficiency

Why Existing Solutions Failed

  • Bloomberg & CapIQ provide data access, not deliverables
  • Tableau/Power BI dashboards aren't client-ready reports
  • RPA tools require IT involvement for every workflow
  • AI chatbots don't understand financial domain context

User Research & Discovery

We conducted in-depth research across 12 senior analysts, 8 portfolio managers, and 3 institutions through interviews, contextual inquiry, and workflow analysis.

🔍 Finding #1: Data Anxiety
73% of analysts manually validate numbers across 2-3 sources. Average 3-5 data discrepancies per report create "verification paralysis."
"I spend more time verifying that my numbers match Bloomberg and CapIQ than actually thinking about what the numbers mean."
Design Implication: Provide transparent data lineage, source attribution, and one-click validation.
⚡ Finding #2: Context Switching Tax
Analysts juggle 5-7 applications simultaneously. Each switch costs 8 seconds, with 120+ switches per report (16+ minutes wasted) plus information loss.
"I have Bloomberg in one monitor, CapIQ in another, Excel on my laptop, and I'm copying data between them. I can't focus."
Design Implication: Create a unified workspace minimizing friction between tools.
📋 Finding #3: Template Inflexibility
While analysts need templates, current ones require 40-60 minutes to customize. This leads to unsafe copy-paste workflows.
"I have 20 report templates, but none match what this client wants. So I copy last month's report and change numbers—it's faster but terrifying."
Design Implication: Templates must be modular, customizable, and version-controlled.
✅ Finding #4: Approval Workflow Friction
Manual compliance review adds 2-3 days; 40% of reports return for revisions (mostly formatting, not content).
"A report that took 6 hours to write takes 3 days to approve because of back-and-forth feedback."
Design Implication: Build compliance checks into the workflow, not after.
🤝 Finding #5: Knowledge Silos
Institutional knowledge trapped in personal workflows. New analysts rebuild work instead of reusing it. Onboarding takes 4-6 weeks.
"My colleague built an amazing dashboard, but nobody uses it because I didn't know it existed."
Design Implication: Enable discovery, sharing, and reuse of workflows.

Design Solution

Unified AI-Powered Workflow

Instead of analysts jumping between 5-7 apps, we created a unified platform where:

  • Data Access: Bloomberg, CapIQ data integrated directly—no copy-paste
  • Smart Templates: AI suggests sections based on report type; analysts customize in minutes
  • Real-Time Validation: Data lineage tracked automatically; sources transparent
  • Built-in Compliance: Rules enforced during creation, not review
  • Knowledge Sharing: Analysts fork successful reports, learn methodologies

Result: 6-8 hour process compressed to 40 minutes.

Key Design Learnings

1. Domain Expertise Matters

Understanding finance terminology, compliance requirements, and institutional workflows was critical. Generic UX patterns don't work in specialized domains.

2. Trust > Efficiency

Even 8.5x faster delivery meant nothing if analysts didn't trust the data. Transparent lineage and validation built confidence.

3. Power Users Need Escape Hatches

Advanced users like Sarah needed customization depth. Junior users like Marcus needed guided workflows. One-size-fits-all UI failed both.

4. Context Switching is Underrated

Most platforms ignore app-switching friction. Consolidating 5 apps into 1 was more valuable than any feature optimization.
