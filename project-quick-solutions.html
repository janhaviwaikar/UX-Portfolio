Solving for the Apps We Use Every Day

A collection of self-initiated product explorations — noticing everyday friction, questioning familiar flows, and designing small interventions that could make common apps feel more thoughtful.

Product ThinkingUX ExplorationEveryday AppsInteraction DesignConcept Design
Self-initiated product thinking

This is exploration, not client work.

This work is purely driven by self-exploration and by noticing issues I experience as a user in apps I use every day. I am not involved with these apps or companies in any way. These are independent UX hypotheses and concept explorations, created to practise looking at familiar products with a product designer’s lens.

Google Pay

Two explorations around a familiar payment experience: making rewards easier to act on, and helping users understand the bigger picture behind their spending.

01
FEATURE #1 · Batch reveal

Scratch All

Turn four separate reward reveals into one intentional action.

01 / 04
The problem

When several reward cards are waiting to be revealed, opening them one by one adds unnecessary repetition. The interaction makes a simple “show me what I got” task feel longer than it needs to be.

The solution

Add a Scratch All action that reveals the available cards together, while preserving the individual cards for users who want the reveal to remain playful and deliberate.

Concept impact
1batch action
4cards in one reveal
↓repetitive effort
FEATURE #2 · Smart categorization

Give rewards a mental map

Organise offers around the kinds of things people actually shop, book and spend on.

02 / 04
The problem

A long mixed list of rewards forces users to scan everything to find something relevant. The value of an offer can get buried simply because the collection has no useful structure.

The solution

Introduce lightweight categories such as Entertainment, Beauty, Health, Travel and more. Categories create a predictable way to narrow the collection without introducing another complex navigation layer.

Concept impact
4+clear categories
1scanable structure
↓search friction
FEATURE #3 · Priority sorting

Show what deserves attention first

Make urgency and value visible through ordering, not more visual noise.

03 / 04
The problem

Users can miss a high-value or soon-to-expire offer when every reward has the same visual priority. The list tells users what exists, but not what deserves attention first.

The solution

Prioritise rewards using signals such as expiring soon, higher value, recently unlocked and relevance. The ordering does the work while the interface stays visually calm.

Concept impact
1stpriority position
2+urgency signals
↑relevance at a glance
Product thinking: the concept does not remove choice. It changes the interaction from browsing an entire catalogue to giving the product enough context to help make a choice. Past orders act as a supporting signal, not the only source of recommendation.
